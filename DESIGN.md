# Debug Runner Design

## Overview

Walking/failure Codi uses the existing five vector-only `codi-{down,up,left,right,failed}.svg` drawings (viewBox 128×128) through `playerSprites.ts`; preserved PNGs are reference art. Asset QA renders at original size and requires foreground-crop SSIM ≥0.98 on all white/navy/blue RGB composites and alpha, plus visual inspection. Current verified minima are 98.95%, 99.40%, 99.48%, 99.42%, 98.88% respectively; exact hashes/method/screenshots live in `docs/codi-svg/REPORT.md` and `comparison.json`. These existing drawings already meet the goal; this verification does not introduce replacement artwork, raster-embedded SVGs or gameplay changes.

`PLAYER_POSES.win` now uses the user-approved crisp celebration illustration at `codi-win.svg`: clean geometry derived from the front pose, with a wink, raised arm and angled shoe, no blur or embedded bitmap. Practice summary and empty-ranking art share this canonical pose. The earlier contour-traced SVG (SSIM 0.9800183) is archived in `docs/codi-svg/reference/codi-win-traced.svg`; that historical metric does not certify the new crisp pose. Original PNG references and the other five poses remain unchanged. Do not create a second summary-only asset entry.

Preserve the established blue space adventure identity and Codi assets. The audience is children using a desktop booth or a smaller personal screen. Thai is the default; Python/item names and Beginner/Intermediate/Pro remain recognizable technical labels. The interface should feel like a playable educational game with clear feedback, rather than an admin dashboard.

This file records existing design context; it does not generate runtime tokens. The user's current instructions and repository handoff govern task scope and gameplay. No Japan-specific market or language scope is established.

## Motion

Phone/tablet gameplay uses the shared mobile-touch policy in `app/utils/gameplayDevice.ts`, including desktop-mode iPads. `useGameplayDevice` applies `.light-motion` to the player shell; `board.css` removes tile cascades/shatter, sprite idle/hop, dust, burst and HUD flourish animations while retaining an 80ms position transition, static guidance, colours and sounds. Score count-up and automatic viewport scrolling use the same policy and snap to their real values. Desktop and Windows touch kiosks keep the established motion. Memoized tile nodes update when the map or board sizing changes, without rebuilding their presentation on each actor move. Gameplay input, retry/advance delays, scoring and deadlines retain their existing owners.

Per-level score feedback owns its 1400ms display lifetime independently of `beginLevel`; next-level assembly must not clear the accepted points/stars. A new receipt replaces/re-keys the existing popup, and run exit/reset still clears it. Touch gameplay skips the popup's blade/number motion so the real points stay readable immediately.

Game buttons use `touch-action: manipulation`. D-pad single-finger taps also cancel their touch-end default and call the existing movement handler once, including zoom suppression during disabled cooldowns; mouse/keyboard keep native click handling. Multitouch, drags and cancellations remain browser gestures, and board panning explicitly retains `pinch-zoom`. No viewport zoom lock is introduced.

Motion makes the game feel physical without slowing play; nothing gates input or extends the existing timings. The focal moment is a level clear: a yellow shock ring and eight yellow/blue sparks burst from Codi (≤480ms, finished before the 520ms advance), the score card springs in with its points landing a beat later, the HUD score rolls up (`useCountUp`, exponential ease-out; decreases snap) and bumps, the progress fill (`role="progressbar"`, scaleX) slides with one light glint, and the next round's tiles settle diagonally from the top-left (24ms per diagonal, capped at 300ms). `LevelBoard` is re-keyed per `beginLevel`, so starts, retries after a shatter and new levels all assemble the same way; `cascade` is opt-in so review/tutorial boards stay static.

Edge wraps read as one ordinary step: within the same 260ms, a copy of Codi slides out past the edge it left while Codi slides in from one pitch beyond the opposite edge, both fading (`translate`, composed with the hop). Reduced motion keeps the instant snap.

Feedback: every accepted move presses the matching D-pad key (keyboard included) and leaves a dust puff and ring under Codi when the hop lands. A wrong key shakes that D-pad key red and jolts the board during the existing shatter. Combo text punches in on each step and runs hot (orange glow) from ×5. Timed runs pulse the clock red once per second in the last 30 seconds. A lost Dead-mode heart breaks. Notifications pop in; the practice "passed" check pops.

Home deals the cards in left to right once enabled modes are known (backwards fill only, so hover and selection motion keep ownership of transform). The setup popup pops step pips and draws their checks, charges the landing pad on avatar change, flips the name plate on dice rolls (not on typing), pops the age plate, and sweeps the Start button once when the form becomes valid. Leaderboards raise the podium third→second→first, drop each player onto their block, pop the crown, slide rows in (capped stagger) and glow the player's own row twice. Summaries pop their metric tiles in sequence and roll the final score up from zero.

Every effect has a reduced-motion path: travel and scale are removed while colour feedback (red wrong key, D-pad tint, clear glow, own-row glow) stays.

## Do's and Don'ts

- Reuse the existing assets, Thai typography and canonical components.
- Keep hover/focus/press/disabled feedback and 48px form targets usable without sound or animation.
- Preserve gameplay, scoring and timing when adjusting presentation.
- Do not add backend dependencies, alternate audio contexts or unrelated UI systems for a small change.
- Verification follows the user's build-only contract: no tests, external design lint/audit downloads or browser checks without a changed instruction. Do not claim visual/audio or accessibility compliance from build results alone.
